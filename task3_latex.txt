\section{Task 3: Hyperparameter Tuning}

Task 3 was about finding the right crossover and mutation probabilities. The operators from Task~2 stay fixed: two-point crossover, uniform integer mutation (\texttt{indpb=0.1}), tournament selection (\texttt{tournsize=2}). We swept a $4 \times 4$ grid over \texttt{cxpb} and \texttt{mutpb}, both in $\{0.2, 0.4, 0.6, 0.8\}$, with 10 independent runs per combination (160 runs total, pop=100, ngen=50). The reported metric is Average Best Fitness (ABF).

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{ABF across 10 runs per (\texttt{cxpb}, \texttt{mutpb}) pair. Best result marked with *.}
\begin{tabular}{c|cccc}
\hline
\texttt{mutpb} $\backslash$ \texttt{cxpb} & \texttt{0.2} & \texttt{0.4} & \texttt{0.6} & \texttt{0.8} \\
\hline
\texttt{0.2} & 0.6301 & 0.6660 & 0.6245 & 0.6442 \\
\texttt{0.4} & 0.6399 & 0.6328 & 0.6324 & \textbf{0.6803*} \\
\texttt{0.6} & 0.6319 & 0.6202 & 0.6007 & 0.6393 \\
\texttt{0.8} & 0.5819 & 0.5800 & 0.5913 & 0.6059 \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\label{tab:task3}
\end{table}

The best result was \texttt{cxpb=0.8}, \texttt{mutpb=0.4} with ABF = 0.6803, though two high-performing runs (0.8827 and 0.8723) account for much of the gap over other combinations. At \texttt{mutpb=0.8}, ABF is the lowest across all crossover values: at that mutation rate, solutions are randomised faster than selection pressure can compensate. Outside that row, differences between combinations are small and no configuration clearly dominates. These values (\texttt{cxpb=0.8}, \texttt{mutpb=0.4}) are carried forward to Task~5.
